    Hour by hour

    Drag, swipe or scroll sideways to glide through the week. Tap a day button or use the arrows to jump to a day.

    TemperatureRange across sourcesChance of rainDays alternate light and dark blue

    About this

    Each hour is the middle figure (median) of every source with an hourly forecast for it: the Bureau of Meteorology (about 3 days), Weatherzone (3 days), WillyWeather, ECMWF and ICON (about 7 days), Windy (about 5 days, every 3 hours, temperature and rain only) and Foreca (today and tomorrow). The shaded band around the temperature line runs from the lowest to the highest source, and the thin line on each rain bar from the lowest to the highest chance, so a wide band means the sources disagree. The picture for each hour is the one most sources show, except that storms show when at least half of them forecast storms, and showers only when the middle chance of rain is 30% or more. The note under the chart names the sources for the day in view. In the cards, rainy hours have a blue outline and stormy hours a purple one.

    Tides

    Tap or hover on the curve to read the tide at any time. Swipe or drag sideways, or use the arrows, to change day. Shaded = night; green = good time to launch.

    About this

    High and low tide times and heights are WillyWeather's, for the spot named under the tide. WillyWeather gives only the turning points, so the curve between them, and the height shown for right now, follow the usual smooth tide shape and are estimates. The launch hint looks for the 90 minutes either side of a high tide that fall in daylight (sunrise and sunset from the Bureau of Meteorology), and adds the Bureau's strongest wind on that water in that time. Shallow ramps and river bars can be tricky near low tide; check local conditions.

    Boating conditions

    Calm under 15 km/h, choppy 15 to 25, rough above 25. The outlined hours are the best stretch of the day.

    About this

    A rough guide for jet skis and small boats, from the strongest daytime wind (6am to 6pm). The best stretch is the longest run of daylight hours under 15 km/h, or, if there isn't one, under 26 km/h. Gusts, swell and local conditions matter too; check the Bureau of Meteorology's marine warnings before heading out.

    Wind and gusts: the Bureau of Meteorology's hourly forecast for a point on each water. Windy: windy.com at the same point. Seabreeze: its forecast for the town. Windfinder: its nearest spot, for today and tomorrow (its free forecast goes no further). Swell (open ocean only) and tide times: WillyWeather, for the spot named on each card.

    Your weather sites

    About this

    Each tile is one site's forecast for the chosen day: top and bottom temperature, the forecast in its words, then chance of rain and amount. The colour along the top matches the site's line in "How the sites line up". Hover a tile (or long-press on a phone) for the site's address and, where it uses a nearby place or a weather model, which one. ECMWF and ICON are weather models (weather data by Open-Meteo.com, CC BY 4.0); the Bureau's station tile is the latest reading, not a forecast.

    About this

    The top row is the Bureau of Meteorology's official forecast for this location, written by its forecasters. The diamond rows are what the other forecast sites show for it. Where a site has no page for the exact place, it uses a nearby one, named under the site (for example, Weatherzone uses Archerfield for Oxley). Red figures sit 3°C or more away from the median of all rows. Windy and Foreca don't publish a chance of rain, and Windy has no written forecast, so it has no picture.
