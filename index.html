            Rain radar

            The radar map loads as you scroll to it.

            Live lightning mapBureau radar
            About this

            The map shows rain picked up by weather radar over the last two hours or so, from RainViewer, which gathers radar from weather services around the world, including the Bureau of Meteorology's. Frames are about 10 minutes apart, and the newest is usually 10 to 20 minutes old by the time it appears, because the radar pictures take a while to collect and process. The time on the map is the time of the frame, in this place's local time. Blue is light to moderate rain, and yellow through red is heavy rain and storms; the pale shading is very light rain or drizzle, some of which may not reach the ground.

            When RainViewer offers forecast frames, they play after the newest radar frame and are labelled "Forecast" with the minutes ahead. They are estimates, made by moving the latest rain along its recent track, so they can't show showers that haven't formed yet. RainViewer's free feed doesn't always include them; the note under the map says whether it does right now.

            On a phone the page scrolls past the map; tap Move map to drag and pinch it, and Done to go back to scrolling. Live lightning map opens Blitzortung.org's own map, from its community network of lightning detectors, at this place; its live data isn't shown here because Blitzortung doesn't allow it on other websites. The Bureau radar button opens the Bureau of Meteorology's page for its nearest radar, or its radar maps when no radar is close.

            Credits: radar by RainViewer (rainviewer.com); base map by Esri, with data from HERE, Garmin and © OpenStreetMap contributors; map drawn with Leaflet.

            Hour by hour

            Drag, swipe or scroll sideways to glide through the week. Tap a day button or use the arrows to jump to a day.

            TemperatureRange across sourcesChance of rainWind, km/h (figures: wind/gust)GustsCloud coverDays alternate light and dark blue

            About this

            Each hour is the middle figure (median) of every source with an hourly forecast for it: the Bureau of Meteorology (about 3 days), Weatherzone (3 days), WillyWeather, ECMWF and ICON (about 7 days), Windy (about 5 days, temperature and rain only) and Foreca (today and tomorrow). Windy forecasts every 3 hours, so its temperature is joined in a straight line across the hours between, and every hour has the same set of sources. The shaded band around the temperature line runs from the lowest to the highest source, and the thin line on each rain bar from the lowest to the highest chance, so a wide band means the sources disagree. The picture for each hour is the one most sources show, except that storms show when at least half of them forecast storms, and showers only when the middle chance of rain is 30% or more. The note under the chart names the sources for the day in view. In the cards, rainy hours have a blue outline and stormy hours a purple one.

            Wind and gusts are in km/h, with an arrow pointing the way the wind blows. They are the Bureau of Meteorology's hourly forecast for its first 3 days or so, then Open-Meteo's (its best weather model for the place) out to a week; a dotted line in the chart marks the change. The wind figure is green under 15 km/h, amber from 15 to 25 and red above 25, as in the boating section. Cloud cover is how much of the sky is covered by cloud, from Open-Meteo. Weather data by Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0).

            Tides

            Tap or hover on the curve to read the tide at any time. Swipe or drag sideways, or use the arrows, to change day. Shaded = night; green = good time to launch.

            About this

            High and low tide times and heights are WillyWeather's, for the spot named under the tide. WillyWeather gives only the turning points, so the curve between them, and the height shown for right now, follow the usual smooth tide shape and are estimates. The launch hint looks for the 90 minutes either side of a high tide that fall in daylight (sunrise and sunset from the Bureau of Meteorology), and adds the Bureau's strongest wind on that water in that time. Shallow ramps and river bars can be tricky near low tide; check local conditions.

            Beach driving

            Good: within 2 hours of low tideMid tide: softer sand, take careAvoid: within 2 hours of high tideDark
            About this

            The usual advice on K'gari is to drive the beaches within about two hours either side of low tide, when the sand is wide and firm, and to stay off them around high tide, when waves can reach the dunes and creek mouths run deep and fast. Driving after dark isn't advised. The times here follow that rule, using WillyWeather's tide times for the spot named above and sunrise and sunset from the Bureau of Meteorology. A big swell pushes waves further up the beach, so the swell is flagged at 2 m or more. You need a vehicle access permit from Queensland Parks to drive on K'gari; check its park alerts for closed beaches and washouts before you go. This is a guide, not a safety service.

            Boating conditions

            Calm under 15 km/h, choppy 15 to 25, rough above 25. The outlined hours are the best stretch of the day.

            About this

            A rough guide for jet skis and small boats, from the strongest daytime wind (6am to 6pm). The best stretch is the longest run of daylight hours under 15 km/h, or, if there isn't one, under 26 km/h. Gusts, swell and local conditions matter too; check the Bureau of Meteorology's marine warnings before heading out.

            Wind and gusts: the Bureau of Meteorology's hourly forecast for a point on each water. Windy: windy.com at the same point. Seabreeze: its forecast for the town. Windfinder: its nearest spot, for today and tomorrow (its free forecast goes no further). Swell (open ocean only) and tide times: WillyWeather, for the spot named on each card.

            Which forecast is most accurate here

            About this

            Every hour, the app keeps each source's forecast for the next three days. For each day it holds on to the last forecast each source made the day before (1 day ahead), two days before and three days before, going by this location's own calendar. Once the day is over, the forecasts are checked against what the Bureau of Meteorology's nearest station recorded (named under the table; it can be some kilometres away). As in the Bureau's own records, the top temperature is the station's highest reading from 9am that day to 9am the next, and the bottom temperature its lowest from 9am the day before to 9am that day. Rain is measured midnight to midnight, the day the sites forecast rain for.

            Top and Bottom are how far out each source was on average, in °C, whether too high or too low: smaller is better. Rain right is how often the source called correctly whether at least 1 mm would fall. Where a site gives a range, such as 1 to 5 mm, it counts as forecasting rain when the lower figure is 1 mm or more. Days is how many days each source has been checked on, as a source that couldn't be reached on a day isn't scored for it. Sources are ranked by their average top and bottom temperature error once they have 7 days checked, and the best figure in each column is green. The bar under each name shows that average error against the least accurate source.

            The station's readings come from the Bureau's observations page (every half hour) and its app's feed (which also gives the day's max and min), so a short peak between readings can occasionally be missed by a few tenths of a degree. The Bureau's own forecast is the scraper's copy, taken every 3 hours. Results are kept for 120 days.

              About this

              Each stop's weather is for the hour you'll be there, in that place's local time. Where the Bureau of Meteorology's hourly forecast reaches (about the next 3 days), the picture, temperature, chance of rain, wind and gusts are the Bureau's own for the place. Further ahead they're the middle figure (median) of the forecast sites' hourly forecasts, as in Hour by hour, and the wind and gusts are the day's strongest from Windy, ECMWF and ICON. Places you saved yourself are Bureau only, so beyond its hourly forecast they show the Bureau's forecast for the whole day. Forecasts reach 7 days, so stops further ahead show no figures yet.

              Tides are WillyWeather's high and low times for the spot named under the tide; the height in between follows the usual tide shape, so it's an estimate. Beach driving on K'gari uses the same rule as the Beach driving section: good within 2 hours of low tide in daylight, avoid within 2 hours of high tide, not after dark. Things to watch are flagged for any hour you're there: storms, a chance of rain of 60% or more, gusts of 40 km/h or more (or wind of 30 km/h), 35°C or hotter, high tide on a driving beach, a swell of 2 m or more there, and the Bureau's current warnings for the area (for stops today and tomorrow). Each place's figures are fetched when the trip is shown and kept for 10 minutes; with no signal, a place you've opened before shows the figures this device saved then. Trips stay in this browser on this device; nothing is sent anywhere.

              Official forecast and station readings: Bureau of Meteorology, through the data feed its own app uses (unofficial and may change), with the scraper's copy as a fallback. Forecast site figures: collected automatically from each site. For warnings and safety decisions, follow the Bureau of Meteorology. Best viewed at 1024 × 768 or wider, or on your phone.
